You enter the gift shop and note how eerily quiet it is. The silence only highlights the pounding behind your eyes. The distinct absence of small feet hitting the old hardwood flooring 
and shrill giggles makes the room feel lifeless; lacking the warmth the memories you harbour of this space hold. 

Surronding you cold metal shelving units overflow with various merchandise. Soft toys, science kits, those robotic dinosaurs that last no longer than a month. You always found this 
particular gift shop to have a clever marketing strategy - Tempt the kids with colourful plastic adorned with flashy lights and bells, welded into thir boxes, batteries NOT included.
By the time the guardian in question has made it through the queue, they've realised they'll need mini screwdrivers, batteries, and industrial strength scissors just to open the thing. 
Conveniently, the gift shop also sells all the former, placed strategically by the tills. Time and time again your trips with Mila had ended spending £4 more on batteries than you
thought acceptable. 

At the far end of the gift shop stood the main entrance. Its glass panelled doors usually bathed the colour-filled room in golden sunlight, but tonight, only street lights pierced the windows.
Logically, you make a beline for the large doors, the sound of your heavy boots echoing off the walls. As you reach for the door handle, your hand does not fall upon it, but back down to your side.
It's in that moment you realise the handle of the door is missing. Not only that, but the whole seam of the door is gone, as if the door has become part of the wall. As you desperately feel around
the now smooth wall you come to understand that this is not a viable exit. 

You pause in an attempt to regulate your panic. You gaze out the window panes of what was once the door, and things look... wrong. Its not...entirely different... but you notice a distinct lack
of both cars and greenery, and now come to think of it, text. The billboards still proudly display their graphics, but their branding, descriptions, slogans, have all disappeared. The outdoors has an odd shimery texture, 
like a dream state. The thought that you are dreaming passes through your mind but no, that can't be right, you were able to read the clock in the cafe. You distincly remember your
psychology lecturer back in college stating that you cannot read the clocks within your own dreams.   

You have to find a way call for help. You check your pockets and find your phone missing.....
